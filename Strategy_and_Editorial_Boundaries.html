Broken to Balanced

A 180-day, carousel-only growth strategy

Prepared: 27 October 2026. Editorial window: 28 October 2026–25 April 2027, America/Toronto. These dates reserve copy slots; nothing has been scheduled or published.

1. The decision

Build an original, text-led Instagram publication for men and fathers rebuilding after separation and major setbacks. Keep the existing Broken to Balanced identity. Use recognition to earn attention, practical value to earn saves and shares, and a consistent editorial promise to earn follows.

The ambition remains the scale of the reference accounts: approximately one million and 2.6 million followers in the supplied screenshots. Those are aspirations, not a forecast for this account or a promise for this 180-day window. No authenticated current follower baseline, post insights, acquisition history, or retention data was available. The earlier 500/2,000/10,000 milestones were proposed stretch goals, not validated predictions; they must not become performance commitments by repetition.

Do not drift: no Reels production, podcast launch, advertising, sales funnel, affiliate offer, new community platform, or paid growth programme is part of this execution. The podcast remains a future depth channel. The current product is the carousel publication itself.

3. Positioning and audience

Core audience: men and fathers rebuilding after separation, divorce, loss of confidence, and related practical setbacks. North America remains the initial context. People outside that core are welcome; the writing does not require hostility toward another gender or an exclusive identity to be relatable.

Positioning: For people who still have to show up while their personal life is unsettled, Broken to Balanced offers honest recognition and one usable next step—without public blame, false certainty or a performed comeback.

Keep existing brand language: “Rebuilding without noise, shame, or performance.” “Still standing. Still building.” Do not replace the identity with a generic success, wealth or dating-advice account.

5. The repeatable carousel structure

One post per editorial day; six slides each. This cadence is a capacity choice for the requested 180-day library, not an algorithm requirement. If safe review and useful quality cannot be maintained daily, reduce publishing frequency while retaining IDs and sequence. Do not turn missed posts into a catch-up flood.

Slide 1: a specific, understandable tension. No false statistic, diagnosis or impossible promise.

Slide 2: another meaningful entry point; a reader should understand the situation even if this is the first slide they see. No guarantee that Instagram will resurface it.

Slides 3–4: a useful distinction, original example sentence or manageable practice. This is the payoff, not a string of emotional slogans.

Slide 5: context, limits or a concrete next action. Safety-sensitive caveats stay in the carousel, not only in a tiny disclaimer.

Slide 6: one clear primary CTA linked to the post's job: share recognition, save a practical tool, or follow the founder's recurring editorial promise. No “comment YES,” tagging an ex, forced confession or engagement exchange.

Each caption adds context rather than restating every slide. Three descriptive hashtags are supplied as optional metadata; this number is an editorial restraint, not a platform rule or a growth claim. Do not use irrelevant trending tags or imply a popular hashtag validates the advice.

No music is needed for this text-only phase. No stock imagery, synthetic portraits, movie clips, fake messages, or AI-generated people pretending to be the founder. AI assists drafting and production; the owner remains accountable for what is published.

6. Truth and privacy rules

Green: publishable after normal editorial approval

Original observations about ordinary rebuilding; clearly described suggested practices; fictional example sentences explicitly framed as examples; high-level brand commitments; respectful reflections with no private identifiers or professional claims.

Amber: requires explicit owner attestation or specialist review

First-person claims, including “I learned,” “I did,” “this helped me,” a particular outcome or a new personal detail. The 12 P6 posts require owner attestation, even when they mainly express editorial commitments. Any health, legal, financial or safeguarding statement beyond ordinary support must be checked with a suitably qualified source; case-specific advice is out of scope.

A source showing the owner asked for help does not prove a suggested technique was followed or successful. A record of a difficult exchange does not prove motivation, causation, diagnosis or fault. The absence of a document does not prove an event did not occur.

Red: do not publish

Private messages or screenshots; children’s names, faces, schools, birthdays, symptoms or quotations; case numbers; identifiable allegations; other parties' diagnoses; identifying financial details; live proceedings; legal tactics; exposure or public adjudication; “all women/men”; narcissist/attachment labels used to diagnose strangers; guaranteed recovery or guaranteed relationship outcomes; wealth-flex revenge; secret surveillance; fake testimonials; invented autobiographical scenes; “no contact” that ignores required communication; advice to confront an unsafe person.

A generic disclaimer does not make unsafe content acceptable. Where abuse or coercion might be present, do not assume ordinary co-parenting or relationship communication scripts are suitable. Justice Canada's guidance explicitly warns that general parenting tools may not fit family-violence situations. This is a safety boundary, not a finding about the owner's family. [R05–R06]

WHO's guide is a credible general stress-support resource; it does not certify this calendar or justify a medical claim. Gottman material offers communication ideas for appropriate relationship contexts, not a guarantee of cooperation or a substitute for safety guidance. The posts are original editorial writing, not reproduced exercises or a treatment programme. [R07–R08]

7. Growth loop without a change of mission

Recognition → useful reading → private sharing or saving → profile visit → follow → another relevant post. This is the proposed mechanism to test, not verified attribution for either reference account.

A clear profile and a consistent promise matter because a share alone does not explain why someone should follow. After they are published and approved, consider pinning BTB-001 (who the page recognises), BTB-003 (practical value), and BTB-014 (founder voice). Do not alter the dates or publish out of sequence without a recorded decision.

Human community participation can support discovery: respond thoughtfully to relevant comments on your own posts; contribute substantive, non-promotional comments in aligned public conversations; request an appropriate collaboration only with explicit authorization. Do not copy/paste outreach, poach vulnerable users, collect case stories, use engagement pods, buy followers, or automate comments. Nothing is sent or performed in this package.

No universal posting-time claim is made. Select a consistent feasible initial window, inspect actual audience availability when available, and test one change at a time. A proposed operational time is not a verified best time. The workbook contains dates only, not scheduled clock times.

8. Measurement that answers the growth question

Primary outcomes

  1. Observed net follower change: latest confirmed follower stock minus the confirmed baseline stock. Compare at consistent local observation times. This measures total account movement, not attribution to a specific carousel and not audience quality by itself.
  2. Post-attributed follows per 1,000 accounts reached: only where the account's native insights actually report attributable follows. Missing is blank, not zero. Do not substitute total daily follower change for post attribution.

Decision-driving signals

Shares per 1,000 reached for recognition posts; saves per 1,000 reached for practical posts. Profile visits per 1,000 reached can help diagnose interest where that metric is available. Use native metric names: do not rename all shares “DM sends” unless the tool distinguishes them.

Capture a consistent seven-day post snapshot for comparisons; 24-hour figures may be an early observation but should not compete with seven-day figures. Record actual publication date and snapshot age. Do not use views as unique reach. A sum of post-level reach is not unique people across the account. Do not invent swipe completion, dwell time, retention, or demographic figures not supplied by the platform.

Guardrails

Zero published private identifiers or unapproved first-person claims; no unresolved safety/accuracy issue; no known copyright infringement; no paid or automated engagement manipulation. Log substantive corrections and remove or correct harmful material promptly. Observe audience fit qualitatively without storing sensitive personal stories or inferring private demographics.

Decisions

Before launch: enter the verified follower baseline; check account eligibility and ownership; locate the approved logo; approve the first seven posts; confirm the owner can moderate safely.

After 14 published posts with comparable observations: assess whether people beyond existing followers are encountering the content, whether sharing/saving is occurring, and whether profile visits become follows where measurable. If the data are sparse, say so. Do not declare a pillar dead based on one post.

After 30 posts: compare medians and the spread of results within post jobs and series, excluding incomparable ages and flagging paid/collaboration exposure. Look for more than one strong post before treating a format as repeatable. Use follows as the outcome and sharing/saving as diagnostic signals, not as substitutes for growth.

Days 31–180: review every 14 editorial slots; do a deeper review at each 30-post boundary. Keep the goal and boundaries fixed, while refining examples, cover wording, layout and sequence. A repeated weak series earns a better test or a reduced allocation; it does not justify rage bait or a pivot to generic money quotes.

The first two comparable cohorts establish a practical baseline. Set subsequent improvement targets from that baseline; no unsupported absolute engagement threshold is imposed. Do not call an observational difference a causal experiment or statistical proof.

9. Research renewal and content change control

All 180 scripts are fully written now. They are a prepared library, not a reason to ignore new evidence. Keep the next 14 slots as the active review set and the remaining scripts as ready-to-revise stock.

For each review, inspect a date-stamped sample of recent original carousels from the two named accounts plus a small set of genuinely relevant rebuilding/relationship creators. Record post date, format, hook mechanism, visible likes/comments, follower denominator if verified, and whether the post is old, pinned, boosted or otherwise atypical. Private shares/saves are unknown unless provided. Public popularity is a directional signal, not proof of why a post worked.

Use both strong and ordinary posts to reduce survivorship bias. Never copy wording. Translate an observed audience need into the founder's own lens and a different example. Check official platform rules when changing production or distribution, not social-media folklore.

Revision procedure: preserve BTB IDs; create a numbered version; record why, source/date, old text hash, new text hash, and owner approval. Update JSON first, then regenerate the readable copy, captions, calendar, workbook and visual outputs together. Do not let Codex silently rewrite copy during layout. The live performance of future posts remains unknown until published and observed.

10. Moderation and publishing checks

Proposed default reply to a private-case request:

Thank you for sharing that this has been difficult. Please avoid posting names, children's details or private documents here. This page cannot assess an individual legal or health situation. Appropriate local professional support is the right place for those specifics.

Proposed response to gender-blame discussion:

This page focuses on behaviours, responsibilities and rebuilding. It is not a place for attacks on a gender or for judging an identifiable private dispute.

Do not promise confidentiality in public comments or present DMs as crisis care. For an apparent urgent risk, use appropriate platform safety tools and encourage local emergency/crisis support; do not try to conduct a clinical assessment. Do not turn a vulnerable disclosure into a content idea without separate informed consent and careful privacy review.

Before every publication: verify exact copy, spelling, safety caveats, author truth, rights, logo, font loading, mobile readability, alt text, caption and slide order. Keep the published asset and approved copy version linked. Nothing in this package authorizes account access, publishing, scheduling, spending or external communication.
